Explore the Universe

From subatomic particles to the cosmic web — explore every scale of reality with interactive simulations and real data.

Mercury

Closest to the Sun

Surface temperatures swing 600°C between day and night. Orbital period: 88 days. No significant atmosphere.

Venus

Hellscape Twin

462°C surface temperature. Dense CO₂ atmosphere causes extreme greenhouse effect. Rotates retrograde.

Earth

Our Pale Blue Dot

The only known planet harboring life. Protected by magnetic field, active plate tectonics, and liquid water.

Mars

The Red Planet

Largest volcano in the solar system (Olympus Mons, 22km). Ancient river valleys suggest liquid water once flowed.

Jupiter

The Giant

2.5× mass of all other planets combined. Great Red Spot storm raging for 350+ years. 95 known moons.

Saturn

Lord of the Rings

Ring system spans 282,000 km but only 10-100m thick. Density less than water — it would float!

Uranus

Tilted Ice Giant

Rotates on 98° axle — essentially rolls around the Sun. Coldest planetary atmosphere: −224°C.

Neptune

Windy Deep Blue

Fastest winds in the solar system: 2,100 km/h. Has a Great Dark Spot similar to Jupiter's storm.

Stellar Life Cycle

From Nebula to Neutron Star

Stars are nuclear furnaces that forge every element heavier than hydrogen. Their births and deaths sculpt galaxies and seed planets with the ingredients for life.

Protostar Phase~10⁵ years
Main Sequence (Sun-like)~10¹⁰ years
Red Giant Phase~10⁸ years
White Dwarf / Supernova~10⁹ years
Red Dwarfs

The Most Common Star

Make up ~75% of all stars. Burn hydrogen so slowly they can live trillions of years — far longer than the current age of the universe. Proxima Centauri is one.

Yellow Dwarfs

Our Sun's Class (G-type)

Surface temperature ~5,778K. Fuses 620 million tons of hydrogen per second. Will expand into a red giant in ~5 billion years.

Blue Giants

Short-Lived Powerhouses

100× the mass of the Sun, millions of times brighter. Lives last only a few million years before ending in spectacular supernovae.

Neutron Stars

Extreme Density

A teaspoon of neutron star material weighs ~10 million tons. Magnetic fields a trillion times stronger than Earth's. Some spin 700 times per second (pulsars).

White Dwarfs

Stellar Remnants

Earth-sized remnants of low-mass stars. No nuclear fusion — they cool over billions of years into cold black dwarfs (none yet exist in our young universe).

Variable Stars

Cosmic Distance Markers

Cepheid variables pulsate with a period directly related to their luminosity — Hubble used them to prove the Milky Way was not the entire universe.

Spiral

Milky Way & Andromeda

Rotating disks of stars, gas, and dust with spiral arms. Star formation concentrated in the arms. Our galaxy has 4 major arms and ~400 billion stars.

Elliptical

Mostly Old Stars

Ranging from spherical to elongated. Little gas or dust means minimal new star formation. Often the product of galactic mergers. Host the largest known galaxies.

Irregular

Cosmic Chaos

No defined shape — often result of gravitational interaction. The Magellanic Clouds are irregular galaxies being slowly consumed by the Milky Way's gravity.

Active Galactic Nuclei

Quasars & Blazars

Supermassive black holes actively feeding on infalling material, releasing jets of plasma at near-light speed. The brightest objects in the observable universe.

Galaxy Clusters

Largest Structures

Thousands of galaxies bound by gravity, embedded in hot X-ray emitting gas. The Virgo Cluster contains our Local Group. Dark matter makes up 85% of their mass.

Cosmic Web

Large Scale Structure

At the largest scales, galaxies form filaments and sheets around vast voids. This foam-like structure spans billions of light-years and traces the universe's evolution.

Extreme Physics

Where Physics Breaks Down

Black holes are regions where spacetime curvature becomes so extreme that nothing — not even light — can escape. They test the limits of our understanding of reality.

Schwarzschild Radius

rs = 2GM/c²

The event horizon radius. For the Sun: ~3 km. For Earth: ~9 mm.

Hawking Radiation

T = ℏc³ / (8πGMkB)

Black holes slowly evaporate by emitting quantum thermal radiation.

Stellar Black Holes

3–100 Solar Masses

Formed from the gravitational collapse of massive stars after supernova. First directly detected via gravitational waves (GW150914, LIGO 2015) — two merging black holes 1.3 billion light-years away.

Supermassive Black Holes

Millions to Billions M☉

Reside at the centers of most large galaxies. Sagittarius A* at our galaxy's center: 4 million solar masses. M87*: 6.5 billion solar masses — first black hole ever photographed (EHT 2019).

Big Bang

13.8 Billion Years Ago

The universe began as an infinitely hot, dense singularity. In the first second, quarks and leptons formed. Protons and neutrons in ~1 minute. First atoms ~380,000 years later.

Inflation

Exponential Expansion

Between 10⁻³⁶ and 10⁻³² seconds, the universe expanded by a factor of 10²⁶. This explains the homogeneity of the CMB and the flatness of spacetime.

Dark Matter (27%)

The Invisible Scaffold

Detected through gravitational lensing, galaxy rotation curves, and cosmic structure formation. Doesn't interact with light. WIMPs and axions are leading candidates.

Dark Energy (68%)

Accelerating Expansion

Discovered 1998 via Type Ia supernovae distances. The universe isn't just expanding — it's accelerating. May be Einstein's cosmological constant Λ or a dynamic quintessence field.

CMB

Echo of the Big Bang

The Cosmic Microwave Background is thermal radiation from 380,000 years after the Big Bang. Temperature: 2.725 K everywhere. Tiny fluctuations seeded all structure.

Multiverse

Beyond Our Horizon

Eternal inflation predicts countless bubble universes with different physical constants. Many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics implies parallel branches. Still theoretical.

Beginner

Moon Phase Tracker

29.5-day observation cycle

🌙

Track the Moon through its complete synodic cycle, sketching its appearance each night and recording illumination percentage.

  1. Choose a location with clear southern sky view. Record start date (new moon).
  2. Each clear night, sketch the Moon's shape and estimate illumination %.
  3. Note moonrise/set times using a star atlas or app.
  4. After 29.5 days, plot illumination vs. time to reveal the sinusoidal curve.

Intermediate

Variable Star Photometry

Real contributing science

⭐

Monitor the brightness of variable stars over weeks and contribute your measurements to the AAVSO international database.

  1. Select a target: Mira (long-period), Algol (eclipsing), or Delta Cephei.
  2. Use AAVSO charts to identify comparison stars of known magnitude.
  3. Estimate target star brightness by bracketing between comparison stars.
  4. Record Julian Date + magnitude. Submit to AAVSO Observer Database.
BinocularsCitizen ScienceAAVSO
Advanced

Exoplanet Transit Detection

Detect planets around other stars

🔭

Use differential photometry with a DSLR or CCD camera to detect the dimming of a star as a planet crosses its disk.

  1. Select a target system from the ETD (Exoplanet Transit Database). Verify transit timing.
  2. Image the target field at consistent intervals. Use comparison stars in same frame.
  3. Perform aperture photometry on each frame using AstroImageJ software.
  4. Plot normalized flux vs. time to reveal the transit light curve. Derive planet size ratio.

⚗ Stellar Spectroscopy Lab

Absorption Lines

Fingerprints of Elements

Each element absorbs light at specific wavelengths. The Fraunhofer lines in sunlight reveal: H (Balmer series), Fe, Na, Mg, Ca. Redshift moves these lines toward longer wavelengths.

Doppler Shift

Measuring Stellar Motion

Blueshift = approaching. Redshift = receding. Δλ/λ = v/c. Binary stars show alternating shifts as they orbit. Used to detect exoplanets via radial velocity method.
